# Free Ops Automation Checklist

**TRACE the workflow first. Automate second.**

In about five minutes, spot repetitive Slack, email, or status work worth handing to bots, and dollar the friction before you buy anything.

---

## How to use this

Pick **one** messy workflow (not your whole company). Walk TRACE once. Fill the blanks. The last step names the single loop a bot should take first.

Loaded rate tip: use what you actually pay people all-in (salary + benefits + overhead). If you are not sure, use $75/hr for an ops or CS seat and $100/hr for a manager seat.

---

## T. Track

Pick one thing that moves end to end.

Examples: a support ticket, an order exception, a handoff from sales to delivery, a status update that always needs chase.

- Starts when: _________________________________
- Ends when (done means): ______________________
- Systems involved (Slack, email, CRM, sheets…): _________________________________

## A. Audit

Mark where information is copied between systems by hand.

- Copy / paste between tools: _______________________________
- Re-keying the same facts: _________________________________
- Status chasing that could be a rule: ______________________
- “Did you see my message?” loops: __________________________

Circle the touch that breaks most often or feels most annoying.

## C. Cost

Dollar the friction for that one workflow.

- Minutes per run (from R): ______
- Runs per week: ______
- Loaded $/hour: ______

**Weekly cost** = minutes / 60 x runs x rate = $ ______ / week
**Yearly cost** is about weekly x 50 = $ ______ / year

Example: 15 min x 40/week x $100/hr is about $52,000/year.

## E. Eliminate

Pick the **most expensive repetitive loop** for a bot first. Prove it. Then the next one.

- Loop to eliminate first: _________________________________
- Trigger (what starts it): ________________________________
- Bot should do: ___________________________________________
- Human must still decide: _________________________________
- How you will know it worked (one metric): ________________

Do not automate the whole workflow on day one. Take the expensive loop, prove the win, then TRACE the next one.
